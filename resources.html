Zoom Host setup

How to start and lock down the meeting so it runs smoothly and stays safe from disruption.

Before the meeting (~15 min early)

  1. Sign in to Zoom on the account that owns the meeting [ Steve / Roger ] and start it from there — this is the only way you hold the host controls.
  2. Open Security (the shield in the toolbar) and confirm Waiting Room is on.
  3. Confirm participants join muted and with video off by default.
  4. Under Security, set Share Screen to Host & Co-Host only, and leave Rename Themselves off.
  5. When the co-host joins, hover their tile → More → Make Co-Host.

During the meeting

  • Admit known members from the Waiting Room (let the co-host handle most of this).
  • Post the Venmo (Seventh Tradition) and meeting-confirmation messages in the chat.
  • Spotlight the speaker and make sure the room's audio carries to Zoom.
  • Keep an eye out for disruption — stop video, mute, or remove as needed.

After the meeting

  • Send meeting confirmations to anyone who privately messaged their email.
If the meeting is disrupted

Stop video / mute / remove the disruptor. If there are several, use Security → Suspend Participant Activities to freeze everything and lock the meeting. If it won't stop, end and restart with new credentials shared only with known members.

Zoom Co-Host setup

The security partner who runs the Zoom side from the second computer in the room — the meeting's second set of eyes.

Avoid audio feedback

The host's computer carries the room audio. Keep this second computer's mic and speaker off (or wear headphones), or the two computers will echo. You manage by watching the screen.

Before the meeting

  1. Arrive in person and sit at the second computer.
  2. Join the Zoom meeting, then ask the host to Make you Co-Host.
  3. Mute your mic and turn your speaker off (or use headphones) before anything else — this is the #1 cause of feedback.

During the meeting

  • Vet and admit people from the Waiting Room. Hold anyone with a blank, random, or odd name until you can confirm them.
  • Watch the participant list — stop video, mute, or remove disruptors, and alert the host.
  • Keep an eye on the chat.
  • Stay in the background — protect the meeting, don't co-pilot it. Keep names and emails confidential.
